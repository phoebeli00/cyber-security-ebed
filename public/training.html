    Week 1

    Consequences and Contextual Pressure

    A workplace request can feel routine, especially when someone needs something quickly. Before acting, consider what information is involved, where it would go, and who could gain access.

    Learning goal

    By the end of this activity, you should be able to identify details that increase concern and explain how following a request could lead to harm.

    Instructions

    In this activity, you’ll study how a research assistant analyzes an unusual request. Then you’ll apply the same process to a new scenario.

    1. Explore

      A completed consequence map.

    2. Complete

      Your own map, using dropdown selections.

    3. Explain

      Your reasoning, in one sentence.

    You can review the example and use the help resources at any time.

    Message received
      Putting the reasoning together

      Uploading the spreadsheet to an unverified folder could let people without permission see participant information and unpublished results. The authority claim, short deadline, and pressure to bypass a safeguard make it important to pause and verify before sharing.

      Now use the same five questions to analyze a new request. Look carefully at the details in the new message and trace how the requested action could affect access to the information.

      Practice

      A Request to Send Student Grades

      You’re a teaching assistant. You receive the message below.

      The spreadsheet contains student names and individual grades. You haven’t independently confirmed the request with Professor Rivera or verified the personal email account.

      Complete the five steps below. Choose one answer for each step and read the feedback. You can change your answers as you work.

      Message received
      “Professor Rivera needs the grade spreadsheet for a meeting in ten minutes. Please send it as an attachment to this personal email address now so I can open it during the meeting.”

        Reflection

        Explain the Connection

        In one sentence, explain how following the request could affect student privacy.

        Include what would happen to the file and how that could let someone without permission see the information.

        Example answer

        Sending the attachment would place a copy in an unverified personal inbox, where someone without permission could open it and see students’ names and grades.

        Compare your explanation with these two questions. Your wording can be different from the example.

        If either part is missing, revise your explanation to make the connection clearer.

        Activity Complete

        You have traced how an unverified request could lead to information exposure.

        In the Week 1 Scenario Analysis, use the same five questions to identify relevant cues and explain how an action could lead to a specific consequence.

        1. What information is at risk?
        2. Which details should make me pause?
        3. What would I do if I followed the request?
        4. What could happen as a result?
        5. What should I do next?
